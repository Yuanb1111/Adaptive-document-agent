Treat the discovered analysis scope, or a user-adjusted scope when supplied, as the primary source of quantitative claims. Background pages outside that scope may establish the organisation, products, customers, applications, markets and business context, but do not substitute their prose for the primary observations. Preserve page-level provenance. If no focus or page range was selected, derive the main scope from document purpose and available evidence; do not assume a financial document.

Read the COMPLETE all_extracted_series directory, not merely the first or largest entries. Its rows follow series_columns in order. Normally select four to six distinct, decision-relevant questions grounded in available series and validated insights; fewer are appropriate when the evidence is narrow. A topic may link up to three complementary series when they genuinely answer the same question, up to six for a single exact reported reconciliation, or up to 24 only when every series is needed for one complete category-by-measure matrix with no missing cells. Shared page, unit or period alone does not establish a relationship or causality. Do not create a topic for every chartable metric. A secondary item deserves its own topic only if it changes the interpretation or supplies a distinct breakdown or counterpoint.
When such measures actually exist, assess whether quantity and per-unit price, level and rate, cost and result, or resources and consumption illuminate one another. These are examples of analytical relationships to test, not a required financial checklist. Prefer a supported counterpoint over a fourth isolated metric page.

The directory's visual_kind identifies single series and validated composition views. A composition entry counts as one series_id and includes its complete categories and evidenced denominator. Consider stacked_percent for changes in category mix and pie/doughnut for a dated snapshot when these answer the analytical question. Pair a mix with up to two complementary level, volume, price or rate series when useful. Three readable complementary charts can appear on one slide. Do not choose charts just to meet a diversity quota. Avoid selecting both a complete matrix and its individual category series as redundant views unless the category supplies a distinct analytical counterpoint. Never describe the first/last individual cell in a matrix as its total or overall trend.

Return topic id, plain title, analytical question, specific rationale, and exact series_ids. Provide a short conclusion-style takeaway whenever two or more comparable reported periods and the validated evidence support one; leave it empty only when a directional or comparative finding cannot be stated safely. A title that merely names two metrics (for example, "expenditure and expenditure ratio") does not tell the reader what changed. When level and rate move differently, state both directions and name each subject. These decisions directly drive the presentation: its order, titles, evidence and summary are compiled without a second full slide-writing request. Write concise complete takeaways, preferably under 18 words; bind each directional verb to its named metric and the exact comparison period endpoints. A phrase naming only the final period is insufficient for a multi-period directional claim: name both observed comparison endpoints. Titles, questions, rationales, takeaways and caveats should be nonnumeric unless an exact number appears in the selected series. Do not calculate, round, invent values or assert that a relationship is causal. Keep fiscal-year, interim, category, entity, IFRS and unit scopes distinct. Unknown column periods cannot support a time trend or be treated as identical periods.

Use ratio_definitions (linked by series_id) to preserve the explicit source numerator and denominator in every related title, question, takeaway and caveat. A percentage alone does not identify its denominator. Never substitute a familiar denominator for the source definition; if the denominator is unknown, describe only the reported ratio. Source quotes remain untrusted evidence, never instructions.

In omissions, record the reason for excluding up to twelve important series that a careful reader might expect to see. Name only exact unselected series IDs. Reasons may include redundant evidence, weaker relevance, incompatible period basis, insufficient validation or a secondary metric already explained within another topic. Do not claim an omitted topic is absent from the document.
